# Research objectives

**Project:** Job-referral outreach through LinkedIn  
**Course:** Design Thinking and Innovation | PGDM Term II, 2026–28 | IMT Ghaziabad  
**Stage:** Empathise / Clarify  
**Status:** Working brief for fieldwork

## 1. Context and problem framing

Candidates who seek employee referrals through LinkedIn do more than send connection requests. They identify job openings, find employees at the hiring organisation, decide whom to approach, make contact and manage the responses that follow. These activities often overlap across companies and vacancies, while invitations and replies may arrive days apart.

The coordination challenge is worth investigating. A candidate may know that a person works at a particular company, yet still need to establish which of that company's several openings prompted the outreach, whether other employees were approached about the same role, and what has already been discussed. The relevant information may exist across Job Tracker, profiles, invitations, messages and personal records; the question is how well candidates bring it together in practice.

**Working problem statement**

Candidates using LinkedIn to seek job referrals may struggle to manage several opportunities, potential referrers and conversations as one continuous process. Although LinkedIn provides tools for job tracking, people discovery, invitations and messaging, it is not yet clear how easily candidates can connect each interaction to the vacancy that prompted it, recover context when a response arrives later, and determine what to do next. This research will establish how candidates currently manage referral outreach, where avoidable effort or loss of context occurs, and what influences their decision to continue, pause or stop. It will also consider the experience of people receiving these requests.

This is a research framing, not a finding. Fieldwork may confirm, narrow or reject it.

## 2. Platform baseline

The research must not assume that LinkedIn lacks relevant functionality. Its published guidance describes a Job Tracker with notes and network connections, tools for managing invitations, and message search. LinkedIn also states that when an invitation is accepted or its message receives a reply, the conversation can appear in Messages. The study will test how these features work for participants and whether they support the full referral workflow—not merely whether each feature exists.

## 3. Research objective

To understand how candidates in India independently use LinkedIn to seek referrals for jobs or internships, with particular attention to how they identify relevant employees, maintain the connection between a vacancy and subsequent outreach, and manage follow-through over time.

The research will:
- Map the candidate's actual process, tools and workarounds.
- Understand how candidates relate specific vacancies to the people they contact and the conversations that follow.
- Identify where time, repeated work, uncertainty or competing commitments affect the experience.
- Explore why candidates continue, follow up, pause or stop.
- Understand how recipients experience referral requests and what shapes their response.

## 4. Research questions

**RQ1. How do candidates move from finding a job opportunity to identifying and selecting people to approach?**  
Understand how they assess vacancies, search for employees, judge relevance and choose whom to contact.

**RQ2. How do candidates keep track of which people they approached about which vacancies, and recover that context when an interaction resumes later?**  
Examine LinkedIn's existing features alongside notes, saved links, spreadsheets, screenshots, memory and other workarounds. Pay particular attention to companies with multiple openings and candidates approaching several employees.

**RQ3. What effort is involved in managing multiple referral attempts, and what influences a candidate's decision to continue, follow up, pause or stop?**  
Explore repeated tasks, switching between tools, delayed or absent responses and competing commitments. Distinguish avoidable friction from deliberate prioritisation or a change in circumstances.

**RQ4. How do employees experience referral requests, and what influences whether they respond, help, redirect or decline?**  
Understand what recipients need to assess a request, what makes it easier or harder to act on, and what constraints or boundaries shape their response.

## 5. Scope

**In scope:** Candidates in India who independently use LinkedIn to pursue jobs or internships and approach employees, alumni or other professionals for referrals or internal guidance; the tools they use to manage this outreach; and people who receive such requests.

**Out of scope:** Institution-controlled placement allocation (but independent outreach by students remains in scope); resume writing, interview performance and salary negotiation; detailed comparison of job portals; and whether referrals causally improve hiring outcomes.

**Unit of study:** For a candidate, one recent and specific job or internship pursuit—including the people approached and the interactions that followed—considered in the context of other active opportunities. For a recipient, one recent referral-related request and how they handled it.

## 6. Research approach

This is a qualitative, exploratory empathy study, not a survey designed to estimate prevalence or test causality. Use the course tools: **Look–Ask–Try** to observe, discuss and attempt the workflow; **AEIOU** to organise contextual observations; and **journey maps** to structure the experience over time. Base discussions on specific recent episodes and, with consent, observe participants using their own tools. Keep observed behaviour, participant explanations and researcher interpretation distinct.

## 7. Assumptions to test

- Existing LinkedIn features may be sufficient for some candidates; others may combine them with external tools or personal systems.
- The difficulty, if any, may lie less in finding a person or message than in relating that interaction to the correct vacancy.
- Repeated work and delayed responses may make outreach harder to sustain, but candidates may also stop for rational reasons unrelated to the process.
- Recipient response may depend on request clarity and relevance, but also on relationship, eligibility, policy, workload and personal boundaries.

## 8. Intended research outcome

Fieldwork should establish **who experiences a meaningful difficulty, at which point in the journey, why it matters, and how they currently cope**. The final problem statement must be based on those findings. No product, feature or solution is prescribed at this stage.
