# 1. Research objectives and problem scope

LinkedIn stores people and it stores jobs. It stores nothing that joins the two. So the candidate is the only record of which role they were pursuing through which person, and that record has to survive a week or more of silence while twenty other asks are in flight. Four research questions test this and the attrition it produces. Each carries a condition under which the problem statement it serves is abandoned.

## What LinkedIn actually gives the candidate

Three objects, in three places, and no reference between them.

| Object | Where it lives | What it does not carry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The person | Profile, connection, acceptance notification | Any record of why they were contacted or about which role |
| The posting | Jobs search, or My Items if saved | Any record of who was contacted about it |
| The ask | Invitation note, then the message thread | Any reference to a posting, and the note itself is not retrievable once the invitation is accepted |

The company is one click from the person, so identity and employer are never the thing that is lost. A company with forty open roles is the problem. Knowing someone works at Infosys does not tell the candidate which Infosys posting prompted the request, whether that posting is still open, or where its link went.

Mechanics to confirm in the Look-Ask-Try session rather than assert in the presentation:

- Whether the invitation note is visible to the sender after acceptance, and from where
- What the sent-invitations list shows, and how many clicks it takes to reach
- What an acceptance notification carries beyond name and headline
- What remains retrievable once a posting is closed or taken down

## The two problem statements

### Problem A: a referral pursuit has no representation anywhere

A referral pursuit is a triple: this posting, at this company, through this person. LinkedIn has no object for that triple, so the candidate holds it in memory or not at all. It has to survive the gap between sending and acceptance, which the candidate does not control and cannot predict, while other asks accumulate on top of it.

The cost lands at the moment of success. The invitation was accepted, which is the part that worked. What the candidate cannot recover is which role, whether it is still open, and what they already said. The message they send instead is generic, because a generic message is the only one available to someone who has lost the specifics.

### Problem B: the pursuit has to be sustained against no feedback, and dropping it is cheap while resuming it is not

Effort per company arrives in one serial block. Returns arrive later, probabilistically, or never. The candidate has no idea what a normal reply rate looks like, so there is no standard against which two days of silence can be read as anything other than failure. They stop, and stopping is free.

Resuming is not free, and this is where the two problems meet. Because nothing stored the pursuit, picking it up again a week later costs nearly what starting cost. So the structural gap in Problem A raises the price of re-entry, which lowers the chance of re-entry, which is the attrition in Problem B. The window in which all of this is decided is also the window holding academic deadlines and everything else outside college.

### Why manual labour is not a third statement

The repetition across searching, identifying and contacting is real, and it is the mechanism behind Problem B rather than a problem of its own. Stated alone, tedium names its own answer, and the exercise then tests a solution instead of discovering a need. It is carried into RQ2 as a measured quantity.

### Surface complaint against latent need

Asked directly, candidates say nobody replies. That is the explicit pain point. Both statements above are latent. A candidate who cannot hold a pursuit together long enough to make a specific ask will experience the result as a reply-rate problem and report it that way. Separating the two is what the fieldwork is for.

## The journey under study

1. Deciding which roles to target
2. Finding an opening worth pursuing
3. Identifying who at that company could refer
4. Sending the request or the ask
5. Waiting, with no signal
6. Re-engaging when someone accepts
7. Deciding whether to continue or stop

Stage 6 is where Problem A surfaces and it is the stage nobody maps, because sending is the last thing the candidate actively does. Stage 7 is where Problem B resolves.

## Scope

In scope: candidates in India who pursue roles themselves on LinkedIn and ask people inside target companies for referrals, together with the people who receive those asks.

Out of scope:

| Excluded | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| Campus placement process | The placement committee controls access, so there is no design freedom, and it is a different system from self-directed outreach |
| Resume writing, interview performance, salary discussion | These sit after the stages under study |
| Job boards other than LinkedIn | The referral graph is the only reason the site matters here rather than Naukri or Indeed |
| Whether a referral improves the odds | Treated as given. The question is why candidates stop pursuing one |

Bounded this tightly so the journey map fits one page and every stage is observable inside a ten-minute session.

## Research questions

### RQ1. When an invitation is accepted days after it was sent, can the candidate recover which role they were pursuing, whether it is still open, and what they already said?

Serves Problem A. Deliberately does not ask whether they remember the person, which is one click away and never the failure.

Design decision it changes: whether the intervention belongs at the moment of acceptance, which is currently unserved by anything, or earlier at the point of sending.

Falsification criterion, first part: if candidates recover the role and the posting in under a minute, the join is not lost and Problem A is wrong.

Falsification criterion, second part: if it emerges that most candidates were never pursuing a specific posting, and connect broadly before deciding what to ask about, then the join was never formed and the problem is not that LinkedIn loses it. That is a different problem statement and it would replace this one.

### RQ2. Where does the mapping between a posting and the people contacted about it live, what does each company cost in effort, and at what point does the mapping break?

Serves both statements, and carries the manual-labour observation as a measured quantity rather than an assumption.

Design decision it changes: whether the gap is the absence of a store or the collapse of one under load. Those findings call for opposite designs, because the first needs a store built and the second needs an existing one made cheaper to keep.

Falsification criterion: if most candidates maintain a working mapping and report no loss, the problem moves to another stage.

### RQ3. How long does a response actually take, at what point does the candidate stop, and do they know what a normal reply rate is?

Serves Problem B. Three quantities, and the finding is the relationship between the first two. The third is the new part: without a base rate there is nothing against which silence can be interpreted.

Design decision it changes: whether the intervention has to hold the candidate in the process during the silence, or has to improve the asks themselves.

Falsification criterion: if candidates hold accurate expectations of reply rates and stop only after the median response time has passed, they are acting on evidence rather than quitting early. Problem B is then wrong and the problem becomes response rate, which is a question of whom they ask and what they write.

### RQ4. When someone accepts a request and then receives a generic message, what do they do, and what would have made them act?

Serves the accommodation requirement from the empathy video by putting the question to the party being imposed upon. It also tests the consequence of Problem A directly, since the generic message is the output of the lost join.

Design decision it changes: whether recovering the specifics is worth anything. If specificity makes no difference to the recipient, fixing the join is cosmetic.

Falsification criterion, first part: if recipients report that they refer only people they already know, regardless of how specific the ask is, then no improvement to the ask changes the outcome and both statements need rethinking.

Falsification criterion, second part: unwanted contact on LinkedIn is reported at higher rates by women than by men. Any design direction that raises cold-contact volume therefore does not affect all recipients equally. If the data reproduces that pattern it belongs in the results, not in a limitation note.

## Traceability rule

Every item in the questionnaire names the research question it serves. An item that cannot be tied to a design decision or a journey-map stage is deleted at Step 3 rather than carried forward.

## Open before Step 2

1. Confirm the A and B split, or say where the real division sits.
2. RQ3 needs a recall measure for abandonment. Elapsed days is the truer variable and the worse recalled. Number of companies attempted before stopping is recalled well and is a usable proxy. Asking both and reporting the discrepancy is itself a finding about how the wait is experienced.
3. RQ4 needs three conversations on the receiving end. Employees at any company who have been asked by strangers count the same as recruiters and are easier to reach. If three are not reachable, the question gets answered from candidate-side evidence alone and is weaker for it.
