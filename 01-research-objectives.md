# 1. Research objectives and problem scope

## Problem statement

### The challenge of managing job-specific referral outreach on LinkedIn

Candidates who use LinkedIn to pursue multiple job opportunities through employee referrals must coordinate three connected elements: the job they are targeting, the professionals they approach, and the conversations that follow.

Although LinkedIn provides tools to discover job openings, save opportunities, find professionals, manage connection invitations and exchange messages, candidates may still need to piece together the relationship between a particular vacancy, the people approached about it and the status of each interaction.

This becomes particularly challenging when companies have multiple vacancies, several employees are contacted for the same opportunity, and invitations or responses arrive days apart. Candidates must repeatedly switch between job listings, profiles, invitations and conversations while remembering what they have already done and what requires attention next.

The effort of managing this process, alongside academic, professional and personal commitments, may result in lost context, repeated work, missed follow-ups and eventual disengagement from referral outreach.

The central research problem is understanding how candidates manage this process today, where coordination breaks down, and how the resulting effort and uncertainty affect their ability to sustain job-referral outreach.

## Methodological stance

This is qualitative exploratory research, not hypothesis testing. The purpose is to understand the process as candidates experience it, in their own terms, before any problem is narrowed or any solution is considered.

Three consequences follow, and they govern everything in the later steps.

No hypotheses and no pre-specified measures. The research questions below ask how and what. They do not propose relationships to be confirmed. Deciding in advance what to count would commit us to a frame built from our own assumptions, which is the assimilation the empathy video warns against. The frame has to come out of the observations.

Observation carries more weight than self-report. People describe this process as a reply-rate problem because that is the part they notice. What they actually do while managing it is visible only by watching. Where an account and an observation disagree, the disagreement is the finding, which is the point of Dr Tripathy's note on inconsistencies between what people say and what they do.

Disconfirming cases are sought rather than avoided. For each research question below there is a note on what we would be seeing if our reading of the problem is wrong. Negative case analysis is how qualitative work stays honest, and it takes the place of statistical testing rather than imitating it.

## Research questions

Four questions, drawn from the central research problem above. Each one names what it orients us to observe, which fieldwork tool answers it, and what a disconfirming case would look like.

### RQ1. How do candidates currently organise and keep track of the jobs they target, the people they approach, and the conversations that follow?

Orients us to the artefacts and workarounds a candidate has assembled: browser tabs, saved jobs, screenshots, notes applications, spreadsheets, memory, nothing at all.

AEIOU: Objects and Activities. Journey stages: targeting, finding openings, identifying people.

Answered by Look, with Ask in support. The participant walks us through their actual setup on their own screen while we record it. We ask them to show rather than describe.

Disconfirming case: a candidate who maintains a coherent record without strain and reports no loss of context. If most participants look like this, coordination is not where the difficulty sits.

### RQ2. Where in this process does coordination break down, and what do candidates do when it does?

Orients us to the moment of re-engagement, when an acceptance or a reply arrives days after the original approach, and to what the candidate does to recover the thread.

AEIOU: Interactions and Activities. Journey stages: sending, waiting, re-engaging.

Answered by Look and Ask together. The Ask portion uses the critical incident technique, asking about the last specific occurrence rather than what usually happens, following Dr Tripathy's instruction to avoid the word usually. Where a participant is willing, we watch them handle a live acceptance.

Disconfirming case: a candidate who reconstructs the context quickly from LinkedIn itself and treats it as unremarkable. If reconstruction is easy, the breakdown is somewhere other than coordination.

### RQ3. How do the effort and the uncertainty of waiting shape a candidate's decision to continue or to disengage?

Orients us to the conditions under which the process is being run, and to how the candidate talks about silence. Whether silence is read as rejection, as normal, or as something they cannot interpret at all.

AEIOU: Environments and Users. Journey stage: deciding whether to continue or stop.

Answered by Ask, through narrative interviewing. Asking for the story of the last time they stopped, then asking why until the account runs out, as the interview slides instruct. Silence on our side is a tool here.

Disconfirming case: a candidate who continues steadily and describes waiting as unremarkable, or who stops for reasons unrelated to effort or uncertainty, such as accepting an offer or losing interest in the role.

### RQ4. How do the people who receive these approaches experience and respond to them?

Orients us to the other side of every interaction in the problem statement. This is the inclusion requirement from the empathy video applied to the party being imposed upon, and the one perspective a candidate-only study cannot supply.

AEIOU: Users and Interactions, from the receiving position.

Answered by Ask. Employees at any company who have been approached by strangers count alongside recruiters, and are easier to reach.

Disconfirming case: recipients who describe referral decisions as resting entirely on grounds the candidate cannot influence. If that is what we hear, the candidate's coordination problem may be real and beside the point, and the problem statement would need to move.

## Preconceptions we are declaring in advance

Naming these now is what allows us to notice later when we are imposing them. Written here rather than buried, so a reader can hold us to it.

1. We expect the unit of difficulty to be the combination of a role and a person, rather than either on its own. We have not observed this yet.
2. We expect that candidates hold most of this in memory rather than in any record.
3. We expect competing academic and personal commitments to be part of why it collapses.

Each is a reasonable guess and none is a finding. If the fieldwork contradicts any of them, the contradiction goes in the presentation, not the appendix.

## Scope

In scope: candidates in India who pursue roles themselves on LinkedIn and approach people inside target companies for referrals, together with the people who receive those approaches.

Out of scope:

| Excluded | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| Campus placement process | The placement committee controls access, so it is a different system from self-directed outreach |
| Resume writing, interview performance, salary discussion | These sit after the stages under study |
| Job boards other than LinkedIn | The referral graph is the reason the site matters here rather than Naukri or Indeed |
| Whether a referral improves the odds | Treated as given. The question is how candidates manage the pursuit of one |

Bounded this tightly so the journey map fits one page and every stage in it is observable inside a short session.

## How rigour is demonstrated

Qualitative work is judged on trustworthiness rather than validity and reliability, following Lincoln and Guba. Four criteria, with the technique we use for each.

| Criterion | How it is met here |
| --- | --- |
| Credibility | Triangulation across Look, Ask and Try. Observation checked against self-report, and both against the artefacts on the participant's screen. Findings taken back to two participants for confirmation |
| Transferability | Thick description of each participant's situation and setup, so a reader can judge where the findings carry |
| Dependability | An audit trail: recordings, photographs, field notes and a dated log of how the coding developed |
| Confirmability | Preconceptions declared above, plus negative case analysis against the disconfirming cases named under each research question |

The Try leg is ours to perform. One member of the group runs a referral pursuit personally across several companies and keeps a first-person log of it, so the group has felt the friction rather than only heard it described.

## Mechanics to establish by observation

Recorded from the screen during fieldwork, not asserted from memory in the presentation.

- Whether the invitation note is visible to the sender after acceptance, and from where
- What the sent-invitations list shows, and how many steps it takes to reach
- What an acceptance notification carries beyond name and headline
- What remains retrievable once a posting is closed or withdrawn
- Whether a saved job can be associated with a person in any way

## Traceability rule

Every interview prompt and observation item names the research question it serves. Anything that cannot be tied to a research question or a journey-map stage is cut at the design stage rather than carried into the field.
